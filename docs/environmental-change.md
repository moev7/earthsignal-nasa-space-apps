# Environmental change → practical next step

The environmental workflow starts with an explicitly selected, completed NISAR comparison. **Use its footprint & dates** copies the actual rectangular crop and acquisition dates into an investigation draft. It does not submit a job. A scientist chooses the domain and runs the existing native environmental pipeline; unavailable sources remain unavailable.

The result story keeps three questions separate:

1. **What was measured?** NISAR gamma-zero HH backscatter difference and source-bound optical indicators retain their quantities, dates, common-valid populations, masks and methods.
2. **What could it mean?** Greenness, vegetation/surface moisture, mapped open-water footprint and surface temperature are environmental indicators. They do not establish biodiversity, drought, flood depth, lake volume, human heat exposure or a cause for radar change.
3. **What is a useful next step?** The domain determines a monitoring or verification question. Field checks, relevant station observations and compatible image comparisons can distinguish explanations. No measured intervention benefit is claimed from a remotely sensed indicator alone.

Cross-result context uses conservative, versioned rules: the actual assessment polygon must be the same rectangle as the radar crop, without holes or multipolygons, and the requested date window must match both radar dates. Matching a bounding box alone is insufficient. Each paired environmental raster must resolve its actual dated source records, compatible quantity/units and matched-pixel measurement. A seven-calendar-day endpoint tolerance is a disclosed workflow convention, not proof that surfaces were unchanged between acquisitions. Insufficient support or mismatches remain explicit; compatible context never becomes causal corroboration or a fused hazard probability.

Environmental indicators retain their own pixel grids and masks. Even when area and dates match, their common-valid pixels are not asserted to be the same pixels as the NISAR measurement. The selection is local workspace context, cleared on identity change. Immutable assessments and saved snapshots retain their original manifests; they do not acquire a hidden radar association.

The supported native crop adapter checks source family, quantity, units, paired method/mask policy, selected-area evidence geometry, raster support and mathematically possible common-mask coverage. Current engine crop records carry the exact selected geometry; a raw provider footprint alone is deliberately insufficient. If only selection boundaries and the window match, the story says **boundary/window matched**, rather than implying compatible environmental measurements exist. **Export investigation** saves the interpretation version, inspection time, relationship checks and original assessment/radar provenance locally; it does not send results to an outside service.

## Complementary predictions

The existing experimental gauge models predict **USGS daily station discharge**, using chronological model selection, separate interval calibration and final-year testing. The environmental workflow surfaces their target and limitations; it does not predict NISAR backscatter, inundation, lake volume or an environmental class. The station’s geographic and hydrologic relationship to the study area needs separate evidence.

The forecast comparison displays the selected model’s measured MAE against every recorded simple baseline on the same test targets. Relative error reduction is `100 × (1 − model MAE / best baseline MAE)`. It can be negative; a zero or missing baseline has no defined relative reduction. Expired, future-issued and withheld estimates cannot be labelled current. Retrospective skill is not a guarantee of future performance or an observed practical intervention benefit. The calculation and release gates are preserved; no additional model is promoted in this increment.

All forecast presentations use one station/quantity/model/evaluation-identity predicate, with issue/expiry checks added for current availability. Scalar validation fields and applicability/limitations must match the unique scored model and horizon. Unresolved records retain their original provenance for audit without receiving a usable-prediction label.

The immediate practical benefit is a reproducible route from a measured signal to a scoped monitoring/verification decision. Demonstrating an actual environmental event and an improved real decision still requires a suitable case, independent reference observations and evaluation with users. Two dates from a small radar patch cannot establish that outcome by themselves.
