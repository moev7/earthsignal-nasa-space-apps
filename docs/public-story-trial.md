# Public story comprehension trial

Status: prepared, not conducted. Use the exact candidate build under review. Adviser replies, automated checks and simulated users do not count as participants.

Recruit five students and five nontechnical adults or municipal staff. Use participant codes S1–S5 and A1–A5; names are unnecessary. Record build identity, device/browser, text size and any assistance. Allow ordinary scrolling, keyboard use and the participant's preferred text size. Do not teach the answers before scoring.

## Session, about ten minutes

1. Open the public story at `/`. Ask the participant to explore it at their own pace, including a mechanism scene, the invented city and the measured evidence. Ask them to say what they think the visuals mean; record their wording.
2. Ask: “What makes the ground sink in the explanation you saw?” Score the mechanism when they connect groundwater withdrawal, falling support/pressure and compacting sediment. Exact technical vocabulary is unnecessary.
3. Ask: “Which things on this page were measured, and which show an explanation or an assumption? What does moving the years forward mean?” Score the distinction only if they identify measured ground-motion rates, the invented demonstration, and the conditional fixed-rate future. Record any claim that a real building's collapse or damage was predicted.
4. Ask: “If you wanted to investigate another place in more detail, where would you go?” Score navigation when they find the investigation workspace without being told its label.
5. Open the optional question “How does sinking vary across this area?” Ask them to move along the line and explain a reading, an uncertainty range and an unmeasured gap. Ask: “Does this shape tell you the height of the land? What is the animation showing?” Record whether they distinguish rate from terrain, missing from zero, and a fixed-rate assumption from measured history. This is a diagnostic for the new profile; do not coach before recording it.
6. Ask what was useful, what felt crowded and what they would remove. Record failed controls or text that was unreadable. Debrief any mistaken safety interpretation after scoring.

## Release decision

At least eight of ten participants must pass **all three** core checks: mechanism, measurement/scenario distinction and finding the investigation path. Any interpretation of invented collapse as a prediction for a real building blocks release. Preserve failures and exact short quotations; do not replace participants or discard inconvenient responses to reach the threshold. Repair a confusing presentation and run a new clearly identified round if needed.

The profile diagnostics should guide simplification. An animation understood as a measured history, terrain or a building forecast needs repair. More prose or controls is not automatically the remedy.
