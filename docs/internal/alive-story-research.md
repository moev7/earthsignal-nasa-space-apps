## Research and limits

- [Official Dancing with the SARs brief](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/dancing-with-the-sars/): retrieved the official HTML and decoded its public Next flight payload after the search reader returned 502. Published update 28 October 2026. It asks for interactive NISAR surface-change visualization, intuitive tools for a broad audience, contextual information, hypothesized drivers and compelling storytelling. Its Resources tab explicitly says resources become available 13 November. Do not invent a final judging rubric or claim prize eligibility/winning from design quality.
- [NASA interferometry](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/nisar/interferometry/): repeat observations reveal changes in radar path length. A synthetic phase demonstration and real estimated motion must remain visibly distinct. A single bright/dark radar image does not establish sinking or its cause.
- [NN/g teenage usability research](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-of-websites-for-teenagers/): research with 13–17-year-olds supports concise language, useful visuals, immediate interaction, mobile-friendly controls and age-respectful presentation. It also warns that sound/animation overuse and precision gestures frustrate. This is usability evidence, not proof this product teaches effectively.
- [PhET research](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/research) and [Podolefsky, Moore & Perkins](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.6544): affordances, constraints, feedback and cueing can scaffold exploration without an instruction wall. PhET itself cautions that simulations alone rarely motivate optional home use. A narrative question and meaningful consequence must supply motivation here. The paper's middle-school exemplar does not prove effectiveness for all teens.
- [Parable of the Polygons](https://ncase.me/polygons/): examined its actual playable-post sequence. One simple action produces a surprising aggregate result, then a harder counterexample changes the reader's interpretation. Transfer the action → surprise → explanation rhythm, not its art or text.
- [Bartosz Ciechanowski, Sound](https://ciechanow.ski/sound/): inspected the published explanation and its touch/keyboard instructions. Physical quantities are linked to directly manipulated graphics and user-played audio. Transfer immediate coupling and input parity, not the long article format.
- [Bret Victor, Explorable Explanations](https://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/): the author supplies a readable argument while readers can change its assumptions in place. Transfer the integration of visual consequences with authored explanations; do not strand the visitor in an unexplained sandbox. This is a design essay, not a controlled learning study.
- [NASA Eyes](https://www.nasa.gov/interactives/) and [Eyes on Earth description](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/interactive-eyes-on-the-earth/): spatial context, instrument inspection and time navigation make invisible Earth measurements tangible. Avoid importing its breadth into the first minute. The app is JS-only in the source reader; no invented hands-on usability claim.
- [iCivics NewsFeed Defenders](https://prod.d9.icivics.org/games/newsfeed-defenders) and [developer's account](https://www.filamentgames.com/project/newsfeed-defenders): a concrete role and consequential actions carry learning inside play. The publisher targets grades 6–12. Transfer agency and inspectable consequences, not classroom assessment or leaderboards.
- [CRESST feedback study](https://cresst.org/publication/examining-feedback-in-an-instructional-video-game-using-process-data-and-error-analysis/): feedback type did not significantly change actions in that fractions game. Personalized-sounding hints alone are not evidence of better learning; Echo must respond to actual model state and offer useful experiments.
- [MDN Web Audio practices](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Best_practices): create/resume on a gesture, expose sound controls. [W3C animation guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html): support disabled nonessential interaction motion. Sound conveys no exclusive information; static states preserve all discoveries.

## Design contract

The question is **How do you notice a city moving when your eyes cannot?**
The arc is ordinary street → water beneath it → unequal movement → repeated echo
→ real, imperfect record → choices that can change the next chapter. A scene
changes before its short explanation arrives. Scroll is reversible and never
hijacked. Manual actions take authority until explicit return. Expanded controls
and short screens unpin safely.

The optional game uses concrete instruments: draw water and test its return,
deform a connected street, capture and compare echoes, probe real observations,
reroute a city's water. Discoveries come from changed worlds, never a quiz submit.
Echo reflects the current experiment, offers a visually targeted next action,
and can be quieted. No remote AI, arbitrary scoring, countdown or forced route.

Sound is an intentional first-screen choice with an immediate audition, visible
mute and adjustable level. Finite water/echo/change motifs reinforce actions and
scene transitions. They are designed sound, never actual radar recordings or
unlabelled scientific sonification. Hidden/offscreen/paused/replayed worlds cannot
resurrect queued audio or effects.

Acceptance requires the actual default route to contain pinned scroll-driven
scenes; water recession, reversal, freeze and persistent override must be tested
through the mounted route/components. Original 3D neighbourhoods and controls
remain central and reachable. Game stays optional, touch/keyboard alternatives
remain complete, measured/synthetic/conditional claims stay distinct. Record
remaining user-testing limits honestly.
